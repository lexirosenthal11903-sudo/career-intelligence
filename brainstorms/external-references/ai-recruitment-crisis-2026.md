_Saved: Session 16, 2026-06-10_

## Why this matters to the product
This article is direct evidence of the market Career Intelligence exists to serve. Graduates are competing ~500:1 for roles. AI recruitment systems are filtering people out at scale. Parents are paying £30k for career coaches to teach their children how to navigate automated CV filters and one-way video interviews. Entry-level hiring is becoming inheritocratic — structured advantage for those who can pay.

Career Intelligence is the answer to this. The £30k career coach, at a price anyone can afford.

## Original post (LinkedIn, shared 2026-06-10)

> In the world of AI recruitment, we know that parents' networking on their kids' behalf is becoming rife. But there's more evidence that the graduate jobs crisis is starting to look inheritocratic...
>
> Last week, the Times reported that worried parents are paying up to £30,000 for career coaches to help their children navigate AI recruitment systems, automated CV filters, and one-way video interviews.
>
> Graduates who were told that strong grades, elite universities, and internships would open doors are now sending hundreds of applications into systems designed to reject them at scale.
>
> One applicant with a master's in AI said he was competing with around 500 graduates for every role.
>
> Parental help has always shaped early careers, from unpaid internships to London rent to useful contacts, but AI recruitment is creating a new kind of advantage: paying someone to teach your child how to "beat the robot".
>
> The danger is obvious: entry-level work becomes less about potential and more about who can afford the training to get past the machine.
>
> Full article by Georgia May Lambert.
